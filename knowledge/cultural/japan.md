# Japan - Cultural Etiquette

## Greetings
Bow slightly when meeting someone. Deeper bows show more respect. Business cards (meishi) exchanged with both hands and examined carefully - never put directly in pocket. Remove shoes when entering homes, temples, some restaurants (look for genkan entrance area with shoe racks).

## Tipping
Never tip - it's considered insulting. Service charge included. Exceptional service already expected. Handing money directly rude - use trays at registers. If you must show gratitude, small gifts (omiyage) from your home country appreciated.

## Dining
Say "itadakimasu" before eating, "gochisosama" after. Don't stick chopsticks upright in rice (funeral ritual). Slurping noodles is polite - shows appreciation. Pour drinks for others, not yourself. At sushi bars, eat fish side down on tongue. Don't walk while eating.

## Dress Codes
Generally conservative. Remove shoes frequently - wear clean socks without holes. Tattoos can prevent entry to onsen (hot springs) and gyms. Modest clothing for temples - shoulders covered.

## Taboos
Loud phone conversations on trains prohibited. Standing on wrong side of escalator (left in Tokyo, right in Osaka). Blowing nose in public rude - sniffling acceptable. Eating on most trains.

## Useful Phrases
Sumimasen (excuse me/sorry), arigatou gozaimasu (thank you), eigo wa hanasemasu ka (do you speak English), oishii (delicious), kanpai (cheers).
