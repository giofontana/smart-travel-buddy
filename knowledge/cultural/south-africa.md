# South Africa - Cultural Etiquette

## Greetings
Handshakes common. Use titles (Mr/Mrs) unless invited otherwise. "How are you?" expected before business - relationships matter. Ubuntu philosophy: humanity toward others. Multiple cultures mean varied customs - observe and adapt. English widely spoken but 11 official languages.

## Tipping
Expected. Restaurants 10-15%. Gas station attendants (full service) 5-10 rand. Car guards watching parking 5-10 rand. Safari guides and trackers 100-200 rand per day. Hotel porters 10-20 rand. Township tour guides generous tip appreciated.

## Dining
Braai (BBQ) culture central - social gathering. Biltong (dried meat) snack everywhere. Mix of African, European, Indian cuisine. Lunch 12-2pm, dinner 7-9pm. Splitting bills acceptable. Wine culture strong and affordable. Try local beers. Portion sizes generous.

## Dress Codes
Generally casual but smart. Safari neutral colors (khaki, olive, beige). Conservative dress in townships and rural areas. Beach casual at coast. Layers essential - temperature swings. Sun protection critical. Upscale restaurants require smart casual.

## Taboos
Discussing race and apartheid without sensitivity - complex history. Assuming homogeneity - very diverse nation. Flashing wealth in unequal society. Not respecting personal space and property (safety concerns). Drinking tap water outside major cities. Ignoring safety advice.

## Useful Phrases
Howzit (how are you), lekker (nice/cool), braai (BBQ), bru (friend), eish (expression of surprise/frustration), robot (traffic light), bakkie (pickup truck), just now (later, not immediately), now now (soon), sharp (okay/thanks).
