# Thailand - Cultural Etiquette

## Greetings
Wai greeting: press palms together near chest and bow slightly. Higher hands show more respect. Return wai unless from child or service staff. Smile frequently - "Land of Smiles" reputation earned. Remove shoes before entering homes and temples. Never touch heads - most sacred body part.

## Tipping
Not traditional but increasingly expected in tourist areas. Restaurants 10% if no service charge. Round up taxi fares. Massage therapists 50-100 baht. Hotel staff small bills. Street food vendors no tip needed. Being generous appreciated.

## Dining
Eat with spoon in right hand, fork in left (fork pushes food onto spoon). Chopsticks for noodles only. Sharing dishes common - order several and share. Don't finish everything (implies host didn't provide enough). Street food safe and delicious. Try everything with lime and chili.

## Dress Codes
Modest dress for temples: shoulders and knees covered, no tight clothing. Casual elsewhere but beachwear stays at beach. Removing shoes constantly - slip-ons practical. Light, breathable fabrics for heat and humidity.

## Taboos
Disrespecting Buddha images or monks (never touch, women cannot touch monks). Pointing feet at people or Buddha. Touching heads. Showing anger publicly. Criticism of royal family (lese-majeste laws enforced). Public displays of affection. Stepping on money (has king's image).

## Useful Phrases
Sawasdee ka/krap (hello - ka feminine, krap masculine), khop khun (thank you), mai pen rai (no problem), aroi (delicious), tao rai (how much), mai ow (don't want), sabai sabai (take it easy).
